Chapter one · the kid

I have always felt too much.

Some kids feel things at a normal volume. I was not one of them. Everything came in louder, brighter and bigger than it was supposed to.

It took me years to understand that this is what makes a designer. You notice. You care about the tiny thing nobody else saw.

Chapter two · the birthday wish

Other kids wanted toys. I wanted paint buckets.

For my birthday, the one thing I asked my grandfather for was a paint set. The kind where little buckets come stuck together in a row, with one brush.

That set was the whole world to me. Go on, open one.

Chapter three · the original designer

The creative genes came from my grandfather.

My grandfather was always making something. If it was someone's birthday, he was the one making the poster for it. Lettering, colours, decorations, all by hand.

I was his biggest fan. I watched everything he did. I didn't know the word "designer" yet, but I knew I wanted to do what he did.

Chapter four · my first project

I emptied the family albums to make a collage.

The memory is faded now, but I remember pulling photos out of the albums and arranging them into a collage. I made one for my father. Nobody briefed me. Nobody had to.

Chapter five · the grey years

Then, like every confused kid, I ended up in engineering.

I never thought it through. Nobody told me you could build a career out of colour and feelings. So I did what everyone did.

And in college, I still didn't know what the hell was happening. Neither did anyone else, to be fair.

Chapter six · the pen drive

Then a pen drive came around, and colour came back.

Back then, Photoshop travelled hostel to hostel on pen drives. I copied it and opened it, and something clicked.

A lot of people took it along with me. Almost nobody kept going. I did. The rest is history.
